Zone Glossary

Reference

A breakdown of each heart rate training zone — what's happening in your body, when to use it, and which activities naturally put you there.

Zone 1 Warm-Up 50–60% Max HR
Energy SourcePrimarily fat (~85%)
FeelVery easy, effortless breathing
Best ForWarm-up, cool-down, active recovery
Duration5–20 min (prep/recovery)

Zone 1 is the gentlest end of aerobic training. Your cardiovascular system is active but under little stress. Blood flow increases to warm muscles, oxygen delivery ramps up, and your body begins mobilizing fat stores. This zone is essential before and after harder efforts — skipping it increases injury risk and slows recovery.

Easy walk Light stretching Casual cycling Slow swimming Cool-down jog
Zone 2 Fat Burn ★ 60–70% Max HR
Energy SourceFat (~65%) + Carbs (~35%)
FeelComfortable, can hold a conversation
Best ForFat loss, base aerobic fitness
Duration30–90 min sustained

This is the sweet spot for fat loss and long-term aerobic base building. At 60–70% max HR, your body relies heavily on fat oxidation as the primary fuel. Mitochondria in muscle cells become more efficient, and your cardiovascular system strengthens without excessive stress. The "talk test" applies here — you can speak in full sentences. Sustainable enough to train in most days of the week.

Brisk walk Light jog Easy cycling Elliptical Incline treadmill Casual swim laps
Zone 3 Aerobic 70–80% Max HR
Energy SourceFat (~45%) + Carbs (~55%)
FeelModerate effort, shorter sentences
Best ForCardiovascular endurance, stamina
Duration20–60 min

Zone 3 bridges fat burning and true cardio conditioning. Fat is still a significant fuel source, but carbohydrates begin to take over. Your heart and lungs are working noticeably harder — breathing becomes deeper and rhythmic. This zone improves VO2 max over time, increases cardiovascular efficiency, and burns a high total calorie count. Best used 2–3 days per week.

Steady-state run Moderate cycling Rowing machine Swim intervals Dance cardio Hiking
Zone 4 Threshold 80–90% Max HR
Energy SourcePrimarily carbs (~80%)
FeelHard, labored breathing, few words
Best ForSpeed, performance, HIIT
Duration10–30 min (intervals)

Zone 4 is where you push past your comfort zone. You're approaching your lactate threshold — the point where lactic acid accumulates faster than your body can clear it, causing that familiar burn. Carbohydrates become the dominant fuel. Training here raises your lactate threshold so you can sustain harder efforts longer. Keep sessions to 1–2 times per week; recovery is essential.

HIIT intervals Tempo run Fast cycling Circuit training Hard rowing Hill repeats
Zone 5 Max Effort 90–100% Max HR
Energy SourceAlmost entirely carbs (~95%)
FeelAll-out, unsustainable, gasping
Best ForPeak power, sprint capacity
Duration10–60 seconds at a time

Zone 5 is true maximum effort — an unsustainable intensity you can only hold for seconds to a minute at most. Your anaerobic system is dominant, glycogen burns at maximum rate, and lactic acid floods your muscles rapidly. This zone develops explosive power, fast-twitch muscle recruitment, and peak athletic performance. It is not a fat-burning zone. Full recovery between efforts is non-negotiable.

All-out sprints Plyometrics Max effort bike Battle ropes 100m dash
